# Dasa and Bukthi: The Planetary Period System

In Sri Lankan (Sinhala) astrology, or *Hela Jothisha*, predicting *when* an event might occur is considered as important as predicting *what* might occur. The framework used for this timing is the **Dasa-Bukthi** system, inherited from the broader Vedic (Jyotisha) tradition and applied throughout Sri Lankan horoscope (*kendaraya*) practice. This article explains the concept, the standard Vimshottari Dasa table, how it is calculated, and how astrologers use it to time predicted life events.

## What Dasa and Bukthi Mean

The Sanskrit/Sinhala term **Dasa** (දශාව, "dashava") refers to a "major period" — a span of a person's life during which one particular planet is considered the dominant influence, or "ruler," over their affairs. A person's entire lifespan is divided into a sequence of such planetary periods, one after another, based on the Moon's position at birth.

Within each major Dasa are further subdivisions called **Bukthi** (also Bhukti, Apahara, or Antardasha) — "sub-periods" ruled by a second planet operating within the larger period of the first. At any moment, a person is considered to live under the combined influence of a Dasa lord and a Bukthi lord simultaneously. Some traditions subdivide further into a third level (Antara or Paryanthardasa) for finer timing, though Dasa and Bukthi are the two levels most commonly used in everyday readings.

The underlying idea is that no planet's influence, whether beneficial or challenging as shown elsewhere in the horoscope, operates constantly. Instead, its promise or difficulty tends to surface most strongly during the period when that planet is "in charge."

## The Vimshottari Dasa System

By far the most widely used period system in both Indian Vedic astrology and Sri Lankan Jothisha is the **Vimshottari Dasa**, meaning the "one-hundred-twenty" system — a total cycle of 120 years treated as the notional full span of a human life. It divides these 120 years among nine planets (the *Navagraha*: the Sun, Moon, five visible planets, plus the lunar nodes Rahu and Ketu), each assigned a fixed number of years.

The nine periods always run in one fixed, unchanging sequence — only the starting point differs from person to person:

These figures are the standard, universally recognized values for Vimshottari Dasa and are not adjusted by individual astrologers — only the starting planet and the exact starting balance vary by horoscope.

## Determining the Starting Dasa from the Moon's Nakshatra

The starting point of a person's Dasa sequence comes from the Moon's position at birth, specifically which of the 27 **Nakshatras** (lunar mansions) it occupied. Each Nakshatra is permanently assigned to one of the nine Dasa-lord planets, and since 27 divides evenly by 9, each planet governs exactly three Nakshatras, spaced evenly around the zodiac in the same fixed order as the Dasa sequence (Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon, Mars, Rahu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury, repeating three times).

Whichever Nakshatra the Moon occupied at birth determines the first Dasa a person runs. But since a Nakshatra spans a range of degrees and the Moon is rarely found at its very start, the astrologer also calculates how much of that Nakshatra the Moon had already "used up" at birth. This proportion determines the **balance of Dasa** already elapsed — a person is not necessarily born with a full, untouched period of their first planet; they may be born partway through it, with only the remainder applying before the sequence moves on. Because this depends on the Moon's precise degree, accurate birth time matters — even a small recording error can shift the reading and alter the timing of every subsequent Dasa and Bukthi in the chart.

## How Bukthis Subdivide Each Dasa

Each Dasa is further divided into nine Bukthis, one for each of the same nine planets, following the identical fixed sequence and always starting with the Dasa lord itself as its own first Bukthi (for example, a Sun Dasa opens with a Sun Bukthi), after which the remaining eight planets follow in standard order.

The length of each Bukthi is not equal; it is calculated proportionally, based on the Bukthi lord's total years out of the 120-year cycle relative to the length of the Dasa it falls within. A Bukthi ruled by a planet with a longer overall allocation (such as Venus at 20 years) occupies a proportionally longer slice of whichever Dasa it falls in, while one ruled by a shorter-period planet (such as the Sun at 6 years) is brief by comparison, regardless of which Dasa contains it. This same proportional logic repeats at every level, which is how some traditions extend the system a further step into Antara or Paryanthardasa periods for finer timing.

## Using Dasa-Bukthi to Time Predicted Events

The core practical use of the Dasa-Bukthi system is *timing* — determining not just whether a particular kind of event (marriage, career change, relocation, health difficulty, financial gain) is indicated somewhere in a horoscope, but roughly *when in life* it is likely to become active.

The method works by cross-referencing the birth chart's static indications with the *currently running* or *upcoming* Dasa-Bukthi combination. An astrologer first identifies, from the placement of planets and houses in the natal chart, what themes are associated with each planet for that individual — a planet connected to the seventh house may be tied to partnership and marriage, while one connected to career houses may be tied to professional change. The astrologer then looks at which periods fall under a Dasa or Bukthi ruled by that planet, or by a planet linked to it through aspect or placement, treating those windows as more probable timeframes for the corresponding event. A period governed jointly by two favorably placed planets (as Dasa lord and Bukthi lord together) is generally read as a more auspicious window, while one governed by more challenging placements calls for greater caution.

It's worth emphasizing that this is applied as an interpretive, correlational framework rather than a claim of mechanical certainty: a Dasa-Bukthi period indicates a window of heightened likelihood tied to a planet's significations, weighed alongside transits and the rest of the chart, rather than a guarantee that a specific event will occur on a specific date.

## Other Dasa Systems

While Vimshottari is overwhelmingly the default system, classical texts describe dozens of alternative Dasa systems, most rarely used today. The one occasionally cited alongside it is the **Ashtottari Dasa**, a 108-year cycle built on eight planets rather than nine (it excludes Ketu): Sun (6 years), Moon (15), Mars (8), Mercury (17), Saturn (10), Jupiter (19), Rahu (12), and Venus (21). Like Vimshottari, it is calculated from the Moon's Nakshatra at birth, but classical guidance treats it as *conditional*, applied only when specific chart conditions are met, rather than as a universal default — so it appears far less often in everyday Sri Lankan practice, mainly as a supplementary cross-check.
