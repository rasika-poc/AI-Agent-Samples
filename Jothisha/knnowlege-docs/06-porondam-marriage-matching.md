# Porondam: Horoscope Matching for Marriage in Sri Lanka

Before a traditional or arranged marriage is finalised in Sri Lanka, it is still common practice for the two families to take the prospective bride's and groom's horoscopes (*handahana*) to an astrologer (*jothishayā*) for **porondam galapima** — "matching of the agreements" or "matching of compatibilities." The astrologer compares the two charts across a set of named categories, each called a *porondama*, and reports how many of them are in agreement. The result is treated as one important input — historically a very heavily weighted one — into the family's decision about whether the match should proceed.

Porondam matching is the Sri Lankan (largely Sinhala Buddhist, though also practised with local variations in Hindu Tamil communities) expression of the same underlying Vedic astrological logic that produces the Indian **Ashtakoot** or **Guna Milan** 36-point system. Both systems compare the Nakshatra (birth star) and Rashi (Moon sign) of two horoscopes to judge compatibility, and both trace back to the same classical Jyotisha texts. However, Sri Lankan practice organises the comparison under its own local terminology and, most distinctively, is commonly counted not out of 36 points but as a tally of **10 named porondam** (sometimes extended to a fuller list of **20**, or a core set of **8**), each of which either "matches" or "does not match" rather than earning a partial numeric score in the Indian style. Because of this, a Sri Lankan astrologer's report typically reads as "7 out of 10 porondam agree," rather than "28 out of 36 gunas."

## Why It Remains Culturally Significant

Marriage in Sri Lanka — particularly marriages arranged or semi-arranged through family networks, matrimonial newspaper columns, and matchmaking websites — has long been treated as a union of two families as much as two individuals. Porondam matching fits naturally into that framework: it gives families a seemingly objective, tradition-sanctioned way to vet a proposal before emotional and social commitments deepen. A horoscope with many disagreements can be used to decline a proposal without directly criticising the individual, which makes it a useful face-saving social tool as well as a genuine belief system. Even today, matrimonial advertisements in Sri Lankan newspapers and marriage websites routinely ask for the horoscope to be sent along with the profile, and horoscope-matching services (in person and increasingly online) remain a visible part of the wedding-planning industry.

## The Porondam Categories

Sri Lankan astrologers most commonly refer to a core set of **ten porondam**, often called the **Dasa Porondama**. Sources vary slightly in naming and ordering, but the following ten are the ones most consistently cited across Sri Lankan astrology references:

| # | Porondama | What It Checks |
|---|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Nekath (Nakshatra) Porondama** | Compares the birth stars of both partners for general health, harmony, and life expectancy as a couple |
| 2 | **Gana Porondama** | Matches temperament type — each Nakshatra is classified as *Deva* (divine), *Manushya* (human), or *Raksha* (demonic) — to judge personality and behavioural compatibility |
| 3 | **Mahendra Porondama** | Relates to children — their prospects, health, intelligence, and the couple's fertility and family development |
| 4 | **Stree Deergha Porondama** | Concerns the bride's wellbeing, health, and longevity after marriage |
| 5 | **Yoni Porondama** | Assesses physical and sexual compatibility between the couple |
| 6 | **Rashi Porondama** | Compares Moon signs (Rashi) for domestic harmony and family unity |
| 7 | **Rashyadhipati (Rashi Adhipathi) Porondama** | Looks at the planetary rulers of each partner's Moon sign and whether those rulers are astrologically friendly, neutral, or hostile to each other, indicating mental compatibility |
| 8 | **Vasya Porondama** | Assesses mutual attraction, affection, and which partner tends to naturally influence or "control" the relationship dynamic |
| 9 | **Rajju Porondama** | Considered one of the most critical checks — relates to the longevity of the marriage and the safety/wellbeing of the spouse |
| 10 | **Vedha (Wedha) Porondama** | Flags potential sources of conflict or affliction between the two charts |

Beyond this core ten, some astrologers work with an extended **Visi Porondama** ("twenty porondam") system that adds further comparisons — commonly cited additional categories include Varna, Nadi, Vruksha, Ayusha, Pakshi, Bhootha, Gothra, Linga, Dina, and Graha Porondama — for a more granular reading of physical, emotional, and spiritual compatibility. A shorter core set of **8 (Ata Porondama)** is also referenced in some sources, broadly paralleling the Indian Ashtakoot's eight *koota*. Naming conventions, the exact count, and which categories are treated as "major" versus "minor" vary between astrologers and regional traditions, so a client may hear slightly different terminology depending on which practitioner they consult — this is a reason to treat the list above as a common reference rather than a single fixed canon.

## Nakshatra Comparison as the Foundation

Most of the individual porondam checks are, at their core, different ways of comparing the same underlying data point: each partner's **Nakshatra** (one of the 27 birth stars the Moon occupied at the moment of birth) and its ruling deity, ruling planet, and associated Gana (temperament group). Because Nakshatra carries so much downstream information — it determines Gana, feeds into Yoni (each Nakshatra is also associated with an animal symbol used for the Yoni comparison), and anchors the Rashi (Moon sign) from which several other porondam are derived — identifying each partner's Nakshatra correctly is typically the astrologer's starting point. The relative distance between the two Nakshatras (counted in a sequence known in the broader Vedic tradition as **Tara Koota**) is also used to judge the general prosperity and smoothness of the match, and several of the ten porondam listed above are, in effect, specific lenses applied to that same Nakshatra-to-Nakshatra comparison rather than entirely independent calculations.

## Kuja Dosha (Mangal Dosha) Checking

Separately from the porondam tally itself, Sri Lankan astrologers routinely check each individual horoscope — and then the pair together — for **Kuja Dosha** (also called Mangal Dosha or Angaraka Dosha), an affliction caused by Kuja (Mars) occupying certain houses (commonly the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th, or 12th, depending on the school) relative to the Lagna, Moon, or Venus. Kuja Dosha is traditionally believed to pose a risk to the longevity, health, or harmony of the marriage, and particularly to the spouse's wellbeing, if left unaddressed.

Kuja Dosha is checked as an addition to — not a replacement for — the porondam comparison: even a horoscope pair with a strong porondam count can be flagged if one or both charts carry this affliction. Traditional practice holds that the dosha's effect can be substantially reduced or cancelled if **both partners carry a similar Kuja affliction** in their individual charts, since the doshas are then considered to balance one another out; various remedial measures (rituals, gemstone or planetary remedies, or choosing an auspicious wedding date) are also traditionally recommended by some astrologers when only one partner is affected. Because of the weight given to it, a "Kuja Dosha: yes/no" line is a standard part of most Sri Lankan horoscope-matching reports, alongside the porondam count itself.

## Interpreting the Results

There is no single, universally enforced cutoff, but the most commonly cited traditional guideline is that **at least half of the ten core porondam should match** — that is, roughly five or more out of ten — for a match to be considered astrologically acceptable, with higher counts naturally regarded as more favourable. Some families and astrologers set a stricter bar, while others treat certain porondam (Rajju and Gana, in particular) as effectively "must-match" regardless of the overall count, since these are considered to bear on the spouse's safety and day-to-day temperament compatibility respectively. As with the category list itself, this threshold is best understood as a widely repeated traditional convention rather than a fixed rule applied identically by every practitioner — practice varies by astrologer, by regional and family custom, and by how much weight a given family chooses to place on the numeric score versus the astrologer's fuller qualitative reading of the two charts (including Kuja Dosha, individual chart strength, and current planetary periods).

## Porondam Matching Today

Attitudes toward porondam matching among modern Sri Lankan couples and families span a wide range. In more traditional or rural settings, and among families arranging a marriage through introduction or matrimonial services, a favourable porondam reading is still often treated as a near-prerequisite before a proposal is allowed to move forward, and a poor match can end discussions regardless of how well the couple otherwise gets along. In urban, professional, and diaspora communities, by contrast, it is increasingly common for horoscope matching to be sought out of respect for parents or tradition, or simply for reassurance, while the couple's own compatibility, shared values, financial readiness, and family opinions carry equal or greater weight in the actual decision. Several contemporary Sri Lankan astrology services explicitly caution against "obsessing over the score," framing the porondam count as a starting point for discussion rather than a pass/fail exam — a reminder that, even within a culture where the practice remains widely respected, it is generally treated today as one input among several rather than the sole determinant of whether a marriage should proceed.
