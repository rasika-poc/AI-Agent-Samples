# What to Check on a Horoscope: A Practical Reading Checklist

When a Sri Lankan astrologer (Jothishayā) sits down with a horoscope (*handahana*), the reading is rarely a single glance-and-pronounce affair. It follows a fairly consistent, layered sequence — starting from the raw birth data and ending with a cross-checked conclusion. This guide walks through that sequence step by step, in the order most practitioners actually use it, whether they are working in the Sri Lankan Hela Jothisha tradition or the broader South Indian/Vedic (Jyotisha) system on which it is built.

This is a practical reference, not a substitute for study under a qualified teacher or a consultation with a professional astrologer.

## Step 1: Verify the Birth Data and the Lagna Calculation

Before any interpretation begins, the astrologer checks the raw inputs:

- **Date, time, and place of birth.** Even a few minutes' error in birth time can shift the Lagna (ascendant) into a neighbouring sign, which changes almost every subsequent conclusion. Time is usually cross-checked against hospital or family records and, where uncertain, "rectified" against known life events.
- **Place of birth** fixes the latitude/longitude used to calculate the exact degree of the rising sign, since the ascendant changes roughly every two hours.
- **Correct Lagna calculation.** The astrologer confirms which sign and which degree was rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of birth, and which Nakshatra pada that degree falls in. Because the Lagna anchors the entire chart, this verification step is treated as foundational — everything downstream depends on getting it right.

## Step 2: Examine the Lagna and Lagna Lord

The Lagna (ascendant) is the first house of the chart and represents the physical body, general vitality, temperament, and the lens through which the rest of the horoscope is read. The astrologer looks at:

- The **sign** occupying the first house and the natural qualities it lends the individual.
- The **planets placed in the first house** and any aspects (drishti) falling on it.
- The **Lagna lord** — the ruling planet of the ascendant sign — and where that planet sits elsewhere in the chart, how strong it is, and which houses it connects to.

A well-placed, strong Lagna lord is traditionally read as a sign of resilience and clear self-direction; a weak or afflicted one suggests the person may need to work harder to express their core nature. Since the Lagna sets the frame, an astrologer typically returns to it as a reference point throughout the rest of the reading rather than treating it as a one-time check.

## Step 3: Study the Moon Sign (Rashi) and Nakshatra

In Sri Lankan practice, the Moon carries at least as much weight as the Sun does in Western astrology — many people identify their "sign" by their Rashi (Moon sign) rather than the Sun sign. The astrologer checks:

- The **Rashi** — the sign the Moon occupied at birth — for temperament, emotional nature, and mind (*manas*).
- The **Nakshatra** (birth star) and its **pada** (quarter), since Nakshatras carry their own ruling deities, ruling planets, and personality associations, and are central to Sri Lankan naming conventions and horoscope matching (*porondam*) for marriage.
- The **strength and placement of the Moon** itself — a full or waxing Moon, well-aspected and not afflicted, is generally considered favourable for emotional stability.

This step is often done immediately after the Lagna check because, together, the ascendant and the Moon sign are considered the two "seats" of the individual — body and mind.

## Step 4: Assess the Strength of the Relevant House (Bhava)

Once the general chart is understood, the astrologer turns to whichever house is relevant to the specific question being asked — marriage, career, children, wealth, health, and so on. For each relevant house, the checklist is:

- **Which sign** occupies that house, and its general nature.
- **Which planets sit in that house** (occupants), and whether they are naturally benefic or malefic for that chart.
- **The house lord** — the planet ruling the sign on that house's cusp — and where it is placed, since a house lord placed in a strong, friendly position generally strengthens the matters of that house.
- **Aspects (drishti)** landing on the house from other planets, which can support or afflict it.

Common examples: the 7th house and its lord for marriage and partnerships, the 10th house for career and status, the 5th house for children and intellect, the 2nd and 11th for wealth, and the 6th, 8th, and 12th (the "dusthana" houses) for obstacles, health, and losses.

## Step 5: Check Planetary Strength and Dignity

Every planet involved in the above houses is then evaluated for its own strength, since a planet's ability to deliver good results depends heavily on its condition:

- **Own sign** — a planet in a sign it rules is considered comfortable and stable.
- **Exaltation** — a planet in its exaltation sign is at its most powerful and confident (e.g., Jupiter in Cancer).
- **Debilitation** — a planet in its debilitation sign is considered weak, like a king stripped of authority (e.g., Jupiter in Capricorn).
- **Combustion** — a planet sitting too close to the Sun is "burnt," and its significations may not express fully.
- **Retrograde motion** — a retrograde planet is often read as intensified or internalised, sometimes delaying results or bringing them back for revisiting, rather than simply weak or strong.

Astrologers typically build a mental (or literal) strength table across all nine planets (Navagraha) before drawing conclusions, since a single weak placement is rarely decisive on its own.

## Step 6: Identify Yogas and Doshas

Beyond individual placements, specific combinations between planets and houses form recognised patterns:

- **Yogas** are auspicious combinations believed to bring wealth, status, intelligence, or specific life outcomes (such as Raja Yoga or Dhana Yoga).
- **Doshas** are afflicted combinations believed to create obstacles or challenges (such as Kuja/Mangala Dosha or Kala Sarpa Dosha).

Recognising these patterns is a distinct skill in itself and is covered in detail in a dedicated Yogas and Doshas reference within this knowledge base. At this stage of the checklist, the key point is simply that an experienced astrologer scans the whole chart for these named combinations before finalising any reading, since a yoga or dosha can significantly modify what the house and planetary strength analysis alone would suggest.

## Step 7: Determine the Current and Upcoming Dasa-Bukthi

Vedic and Sri Lankan astrology place heavy emphasis on timing, not just potential. The prevailing planetary period system (Dasa-Bukthi, most commonly the Vimshottari Dasa) tells the astrologer *when* a chart's promises are likely to activate. The astrologer identifies:

- The **current Mahadasa** (major period) and **Bukthi/Antardasa** (sub-period) ruling planet.
- **Upcoming period changes** relevant to the question at hand (for example, marriage timing is checked against periods ruled by the 7th lord or Venus).

The mechanics of calculating and interpreting Dasa-Bukthi periods are detailed separately in a dedicated guide in this knowledge base; the point to note here is that this step is what converts a static chart reading into a timeline of likely events.

## Step 8: Review Transits (Gochara)

Natal chart strength is then checked against the current sky — the transiting positions of planets over the natal (birth) chart, known as Gochara. Particular attention goes to the slower-moving planets, since their transits last long enough to define entire life phases:

- **Saturn (Shani)** transits, including **Sade Sati** — the roughly 7.5-year period when Saturn transits the 12th, 1st, and 2nd houses counted from the natal Moon sign — which is widely tracked in Sri Lankan practice as a period of restructuring, responsibility, and karmic reckoning.
- **Jupiter (Guru)** transits, generally read for expansion, opportunity, and guidance, especially over the natal Moon and Lagna.
- **The Rahu-Kethu axis**, whose roughly 18-month sign changes are checked against natal houses for sudden change, upheaval, or karmic themes.

Transits are read as modifiers layered on top of the natal promise and the active Dasa-Bukthi period — not as standalone predictors.

## Step 9: Cross-Verify Before Concluding

Finally, experienced astrologers rarely commit to a prediction from a single indicator. The last step in the checklist is deliberately looking for agreement across multiple, independent techniques before drawing a conclusion:

- Does the house and house-lord analysis agree with the Dasa-Bukthi timing?
- Do the relevant Yogas or Doshas reinforce or contradict what the planetary strengths suggest?
- Does the divisional chart relevant to the question (such as the Navamsa for marriage) support the main birth chart's indication?
- Do current transits align with, or delay, what the period system suggests?

Only when several of these independent threads point in the same direction is a firm prediction typically offered. When they conflict, a careful astrologer will usually describe the tension rather than force a single answer — treating disagreement between methods as a signal to look more closely, not to guess.
